# CULL: art-direction and game-feel review (12 Oct 2026)

Repo: AieatAssam/cull-game @ `0cde5ea` (cloned read-only to `/workspace/cull-game`). Built locally (`vite build`) and played in headless Chrome via Playwright (`/tmp/pw/cull.mjs`, `cull2.mjs`, `cull3.mjs`). Shots in `/workspace/cull-shots/`.

## 1. What the game is
- **Stated intent** (`docs/CULL.md` §1): "Nothing exists unless it's on screen. You hold the camera." You're the camera operator; the Talent walks by themselves and "whatever isn't in the shot doesn't exist". Culling is death, LOD is physics (a far urchin "*is* a harmless cube"), streaming is spawning. "Three-to-seven-minute runs… Every run leaves you a 9-frame contact sheet."
- **Loop:** pan/zoom/rotate a 1:1 orthographic gate (W 10–48 u, shown as 85–24 mm) so the Talent stays in frame. Hazards you frame out are CUT for good. Zoom changes hazard lethality (Urchin lethal only at tier ≤1, `src/sim/step.ts:210`). Collect Reels (+15 s film, `tuning.ts reelFilm`) at Close/Medium.
- **Controls** (`src/main.ts:782-818`): WASD/arrows/drag pan; `+`/`=`/`x` and `-`/`z`/wheel/slider zoom; Q/E rotate in 45° steps; Space or HOLD button stops the Talent; Shift steady; P/Esc pause.
- **Lose** (`step.ts`): lost-talent (1 s off-screen), hit, fell (cut Glass/Bridge), out-of-film, red-monitor. **Win:** 'wrapped' = reaching path end.
- **Progression:** default is one Backlot scene. `?shoot=1` gives 3 scenes (Backlot → Miniature City → Night Shoot) plus a 40 s finale. Grades S/A/B/C, 3 film stocks, 5 lenses unlocked by feats, a daily seed, filmography, replay URLs and a contact-sheet PNG.
- **Self-reported health** (`docs/PROGRESS.md`): the kill criterion fails, with the smart bot at 0.90× the passive TrackerBot (needed 1.3×). M5: Tracker completes **100%** (target ≤40%), Adaptive/Tracker **0.936×**. M6: 7/20 Shoots finish. "Human fun test is pending."

## 2. Tech and art pipeline
- TypeScript + Vite 6 + **three.js 0.174** (the only runtime dependency). WebGL orthographic camera at 55° pitch, deterministic 60 Hz sim, adaptive DPR. JS is about 160 KB gzip.
- **Art:** 22 painted raster PNGs, loaded as textured quads and sprites (`src/render/sprite-assets.ts`). Sources and previews are in `artwork/`. Set and terrain are **procedural flat-colour three.js boxes and planes** (`main.ts:248-278`: floor plane, path box `#d2c8b6`, blocker boxes 0.65×0.5). No shaders beyond MeshBasic. Film stocks are LUT-style recolours.
- **UI:** DOM/CSS overlays (`src/style.css`, 12 minified lines). Fonts are system fonts only: `'Courier New'` monospace for everything and `Impact` for the logo and end title. No webfont is bundled.
- **Audio:** WebAudio **oscillators only** (`src/audio/director.ts`: sine, square and saw per entity, a 64 Hz sine pulse at 96 BPM). There are no sample files in the repo.
- **Resolution:** the gate is CSS-sized: 368×368 px at 1280×720 (**29% of width, 15% of screen area**) and 314×314 at 412×915.

## 6. Art-director and game-designer read
**Identity.** "Off-screen things are deleted, zoom sets lethality" is genuinely novel. It's the Superhot or Baba-style rule twist the design doc aims for, and the diegetic camera-department HUD (REC dot, mm readout, thirds, slate) is a good frame. But the art doesn't express the rule:
- Nothing is visibly *cut*. Feedback is a 0.12 s orange inset glow on the whole gate (`style.css .cut-flash`).
- The design's "money shot", a hazard half-deleted at the gate edge with a film-burn sliced face (CULL.md §9), isn't drawn.
- LOD as physics is shown by swapping between four tiny urchin pictures.
- The miniature film set reads as a beige spreadsheet of boxes.
- The painted puppets (`artwork/roster-preview.png`) are charming but generic "cosy painterly AI" with no shared ramp. They sit on flat procedural boxes, so the cast and the set come from two different games.

**Visual quality.**
- Readability: at default zoom everything is 10–20 px blobs on a ΔE<5 beige ground.
- Hierarchy: the HUD (10px Courier, `style.css` lines 1–12) competes with a gate that takes only 15% of the screen.
- Consistency: painted sprites, flat boxes and debug-cyan AABBs give three different visual languages.
- Juice is near zero: no screenshake, particles or hit-stop, linear camera, and sine-wave audio.

**Fun.**
- The 30-second hook fails. You get no title, no goal statement and no tutorial chunk. The default pan is 14× the Talent's speed, so the first natural action (hold a direction) loses in 2.3 s, and doing nothing loses in 8.5 s.
- Once you learn to feather, the loop is passive tracking, and the metronome proves it.
- Decisions should come from the "tension web" (zoom wide vs close), but at default zoom the Urchin is already defanged and Reels can't be collected. The optimal play is to sit wide and track.
- Cuts happen to you rather than being chosen: 24 cuts with zero intent.
- Pacing: a single flat 90-second strip by default; the escalating Shoot is behind a URL flag.
- Replayability hooks (lenses, stocks, daily, contact sheet) are well built but sit on top of an unproven core.

**Top 10 problems** (ranked by impact on fun and identity):
1. **The core rule doesn't create decisions.** A blind metronome wins (87.9 s, 24 cuts), Tracker completes 100%, Adaptive/Tracker is 0.936×, and the self-declared kill criterion is failing (`PROGRESS.md`).
2. **The camera is twitchy and uncontrollable.** Pan is 36 u/s against a 2.5 u/s Talent (14×), there's no follow assist or easing, and holding a key loses in 2.3 s.
3. **There's no hook or onboarding.** It opens mid-take, the first text is "urchin defanged", and idle loses in 8.5 s.
4. **The cast is invisible at play size.** The Talent is about 16 px at the default zoom in a gate that's 15% of the screen area.
5. **The playfield is one beige.** The gate quantises to 5 beiges, Backlot and City look identical, and the 14-colour identity palette doesn't show.
6. **Cutting, the signature verb, has no spectacle.** One 0.12 s CSS glow, with no sliced-edge art, burn, snip particle or hit-stop.
7. **Juice and audio are placeholder.** 0 screenshake or particle code paths, 0 audio samples, and the signature sounds in CULL.md §11 (tape splice, clapper, ratchet) are all oscillators.
8. **The art is incoherent.** 358k-colour painted sprites sit on 3-colour procedural boxes with debug-cyan AABBs. There's 1 frame per character: no walk, no HOLD brace, no tumble. Wrap art is missing.
9. **Structure is hidden.** The 3-scene Shoot, the biome ramp and the finale are behind `?shoot=1`, and the default is a single strip.
10. **The HUD is noisy and has small bugs.** 8–10 px Courier, the film value overlaps its bar and overflows it (131 s), the call sheet is always empty, and the end card leads with jargon (grade math, lens feats).

## 7. Proposals
### Art-direction concepts
**A. "Dailies": 16 mm stop-motion on a tabletop set.** *Pitch:* a Laika and Wes Anderson miniature, shot through a battered Bolex.
- Palette: plywood `#c9a46b`, felt green `#3f6b4f`, carmine Talent `#c8322a`, brass `#e2b04a`, ink `#1e1a17`, warm key `#ffd9a0`, cool fill `#5d7c99`.
- Shape language: chunky, slightly wonky handmade forms with visible seams, pins and tape. Terrain is rebuilt as cardboard flats, cork-board ground and paper buildings with a top-left key and soft contact shadows.
- Identity: frame-outs are physically *cut*. The off-frame part of a sprite is sliced along the gate line and peels away like a film strip, with a burned edge `#ff7a1a`.
- Sits next to: *Tearaway*, *Lumino City*, *Harold Halibut*, *Unpacking*.

**B. "Render Pass": the GPU debugger as the world.** *Pitch:* you're inside a frame-debugger. Off-screen means un-rendered.
- Palette: void `#0b0d12`, wireframe cyan `#3fd8ff`, LOD amber `#ffb000`, cull magenta `#ff2e88`, z-buffer greys `#2a2f3a`–`#8a93a6`, Talent the only warm full colour `#ff5a3c`.
- Shape language: the world is wireframe until framed, and lighting and texture fill in by LOD tier. Things cut at the edge dissolve into scanline voxels.
- This leans fully into the existing AABB and LOD HUD and makes "LOD is physics" literally visible.
- Sits next to: *Superhot*, *Hyper Light Drifter*'s glow discipline, *Mini Metro*'s clarity, *Antichamber*.

**C. "Night Shoot noir": one light, one frame.** *Pitch:* the gate is the only lit thing in a black soundstage.
- Palette: black stage `#0a0908`, work-light sodium `#ffb347`, gaffer-tape blue `#2f5fb3`, rumour violet `#6d6487`, Talent carmine `#e0342a`, clapper white `#f2efe6`.
- Shape language: hard silhouettes and rim light. Everything outside the gate is literal darkness, with call-sheet chalk marks for hazards.
- High contrast fixes readability at 16 px.
- Sits next to: *Inside*, *Limbo*, *Return of the Obra Dinn* (for the one-rule aesthetic), *Gris*.

My pick is **A for the set plus B's cut and LOD effects**. That keeps the painted cast and gives the rule a signature visual.

### Gameplay and juice changes (small to big)
1. Fix the HUD overflow; lift the minimum font size to 12 px; grow the gate to about 70% of screen height on desktop.
2. Lower the pan cap to about 3–4× Talent speed and add ease-in/out. Add a soft auto-follow "dead zone" so you lose the Talent by choice, not by twitch.
3. A title card plus a 20-second tutorial chunk: "keep her in shot", "zoom in to grab the reel", "frame the urchin out to cut it".
4. Cut juice: a slice line along the gate edge, a burned-edge sprite peel, a tape-snip sample, 60–80 ms hit-stop, small screenshake, and a "✂ CUT" stamp that flies to the call sheet.
5. Real sound: samples for snip, clapper, sprocket ratchet and projector hum pitched to pan speed. Keep the in-frame-stems idea; it's great.
6. Bigger readable cast: scale the Talent to about 40 px at the default zoom, add walk, HOLD and tumble frames, and a carmine-only Talent rule.
7. Make the call sheet actually populate, so pop-ins become choices rather than ambushes.
8. Make cuts *chosen*: deliberate cut combos and close-call scoring (cut within 1.5 u gives a multiplier), and Reels that only spawn in risky framings. That breaks the metronome strategy. Re-run the M5 bots and target Tracker ≤40%.
9. Make the 3-scene Shoot the default, with distinct biome art and new hazards introduced one at a time.
10. Bigger: scripted "shot list" goals per scene ("get a close-up of the Talent with the boulder in frame") that turn framing into authored composition puzzles. That's the strongest identity lever: the camera as an author, not a babysitter.
