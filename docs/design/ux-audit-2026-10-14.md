## Verdict

The viewfinder is the hero in theory, but in practice it is a 440 px square boxed in by roughly 30 other labels on desktop. On a phone it gets about 55% of the height. Four text channels describe the same moment: the gate label, the frame message, the call sheet and the touch hint. Pause has no visible pause screen, just the word "PAUSED" in 13 px in a corner. The results card offers 7 actions and 3 lines of overlapping stats. The film-strip motif runs vertically while the take moves horizontally. The game looks like a debug dashboard wrapped around a good toy.

## 2. Sloppy tells

- **Redundant labels.** "CAMERA DEPARTMENT", "VIEWFINDER · GATE 1:1", "55° TABLETOP", "ROLLING", "Slate / CAM 01", "CALL SHEET", "STOCK". Each names something the player can already see or doesn't care about mid-take.
- **Debug data on the play screen.** SEED, BEST —, TAKE 01, timecode *and* film seconds (two clocks), focal mm.
- **Minimap box.** A dashed 84x64 rectangle with a "□" glyph. It reads as a missing image.
- **Permanent key legend.** It takes the full bottom row on desktop, every take, forever.
- **Stock dropdown.** A native `<select>` in the top bar (the largest control on mobile, 25% of the header) for a choice made between takes.
- **Four message channels for one moment.** `#lod` ("Medium shot · spikes dull"), `#message` ("Medium shot. The spikes are dull." — the same sentence again), `#call-sheet` (4 lines; "Coming in · spiny ball" twice), the touch hint ("DRAG TO FRAME / PINCH TO ZOOM"), plus the tutorial card in tutorial state. At cut-stamp the message ("Nothing outside the frame to cut…") and the call sheet ("Nothing waiting just outside the frame.") say the same thing in different words.
- **Type sizes.** 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 28, 36, clamp(22–30) and clamp(48–84) px: 14+ sizes. `.shutter-row` drops to 11 px on mobile and `.lost-banner small` / `.lost-arrow b` are 11–12 px, which violates the style guide's "nothing smaller than 13 px".
- **Borders everywhere.** Header rule, footer rule, call-sheet rule, kbd boxes, sound/daily/pause/left-hand/speed boxes (two different border colours, `#8a7562` and `#e2b04a88`), the dashed minimap, the tutorial card, the lost banner (2 px paper border on carmine), threat labels, and every results button.
- **All-caps by default.** Nearly every label is typed in caps in the source strings, which also hurts screen readers. Caps tracking varies: 0.04, 0.06, 0.08, 0.1, 0.12, 0.16 and 0.22em.
- **Tiny, low-contrast text.** `--muted #d9cbb6` is fine, but the gate label and shutter row are 12 px. "RECHARGING" in muted on a dark bar and the `#b9a990` "too far" stamp are below comfortable reading. The orange `READY` (Burn on stage) at 12 px is about 6:1, which passes, but it is tiny.
- **Uneven spacing.** Margins of 12/16/18/20 px. The left column floats a slate at the top and a hint plus minimap at the bottom with a void between them. The right column does the same with Slate/CAM 01 and the call sheet. On mobile the sound/daily/take row has three different gaps, and the sound popover covers the gate label.
- **Pause is invisible.** The world freezes, and the only change is "ROLLING" → "PAUSED" in 13 px in the corner. There is no menu, dim or resume affordance.
- **Results card.** The headline "HIT" is ambiguous (hit what?). "1 cuts" is ungrammatical. The score appears 3 times in 3 formats. The primary CTA "NEW TAKE ↗" uses an external-link arrow and carmine (the danger/talent colour). There are 7 buttons with equal weight, and the card covers the gate while leaving the HUD alive around it.
- **Title.** There is no title screen: the wordmark is stamped over a dimmed gate while the full HUD (two clocks, stock, sound, legend) is live around it. The tutorial card ("1/4") is fused onto the same screen.
- **Stray stripe.** A dark 12 px column runs inside the gate's left edge, between the sprockets and the image, in every state.
- **Duplicate CSS.** `@keyframes stamp` is defined twice (`style.css:210` and `:415`). The second silently wins.

## 3. Visual consistency audit

### 3.1 Orientation and motion direction
- The film-strip sprockets (`.sprockets`) run **vertically** down the left and right of the gate, as if film travels top to bottom. But the Talent walks and the take scrolls **horizontally** (left → right), and the film bar drains horizontally. The motif contradicts the motion: a real gate's perforations run parallel to film travel.
- The sprockets are static. They never advance, so they read as a striped border, not as film.
- **Fix (recommended):** drop the sprocket border. Express "film" once, as a thin horizontal perforated strip along the **bottom** edge of the frame that is also the film-remaining meter, and that advances (background-position) at a rate tied to burn, frozen when paused. Optionally add a matching top strip for the take score and timecode. That gives one horizontal axis for motion, film and time.
- **Portrait:** on phones the gate is a square in a tall screen. Let the gate take the full width and up to about 62% of the height (a 4:5 crop is fine; it is a render crop, not a sim change, *only if* the world-space visible extent is unchanged). Otherwise keep 1:1 and give the freed vertical space to the thumb zone, not to text.

### 3.2 Light direction
- The style guide sets one warm key from the **upper left**, so contact shadows fall lower right and cast highlights sit top left.
- Observed: the cardboard flats' shadows fall correctly toward the lower right. But the gate's own shadow (`box-shadow` in the gate frame) falls straight down/around, and the cut stamp's `text-shadow: 0 2px 0` falls straight down. UI depth cues should follow the key: shift UI shadows to `+x,+y` (e.g. `2px 2px`) or drop them.
- The pale top/bottom bands inside the gate are the sky backdrop at 55°. In a square gate they read as letterbox bars, which competes with the anamorphic lens's real letterbox. Note for render (not a change here): gradient the sky into the table.

### 3.3 Perspective
- The world is a 55° orthographic tabletop. The UI is flat, front-on paper and kbd chips, and the warning banners are flat rectangles pasted over the 3D table at the centre of view, so they occlude the Talent (lost-her, 1280: the banner sits right beside her).
- **Principle:** the world is in perspective; the UI lives on the **glass** (the viewfinder) and never pretends to be on the table. Screen-space overlays hug the frame edges and corners; the centre stays clear. Only in-world markers (the threat arrows, the "HER" tag) may sit at world positions, and they should point from the edge, not float mid-frame.

### 3.4 Metaphor clashes
The current screens mix:
1. **Film strip**: sprockets, film stock, burn, splice.
2. **Paper production office**: slate, call sheet, "CAMERA DEPARTMENT", dailies.
3. **Video/CRT/digital**: timecode 01:44, the thirds grid, the "REC" dot, the shutter "RECHARGING" bar (game UI), threat arrows, the 75%-speed pill.
4. **Rubber stamp**: the MISSED/LOST HER/CUT stamps in Fraunces on carmine.

**Choice:** one camera department. The *viewfinder* (glass overlays: corners, thirds, REC, film counter) is the in-take language. *Paper* (slate, dailies) is used only off-take: title, pause and results. Film is expressed through the footage counter and burn edge, not a decorative border. Stamps are kept only for the CUT moment (it is a splice), not for warnings.

### 3.5 Outline weights
- Style guide: ink outlines ~4 px at 128 px source (≈1–1.5 px at play scale). The Talent sprite has a white sticker outline in the before shots, while hazards have ink outlines. The scraps have a 2 px ink outline, the UI has 1 px borders, and the lost banner has a 2 px paper border.
- **Fix:** UI uses no outlines. Fill and spacing do the work, with a 2 px Paper/Burn line used only for meters and the frame corners. Flag the Talent sticker outline to art (sprite change, render-only).

### 3.6 Palette drift vs `docs/art/style-guide.md`
Off-palette colours in `style.css`: `#ff4b3e` (drain text, drain outline), `#ff3b2e` (threat glow), `#8a7562` (borders, kbd), `#b9a990` (weak stamp), `#d9cbb6` (`--muted`; acceptable as a paper tint but undocumented), `white` (end-card button text), `rgba(30,26,23,…)` (fine; ink alpha), and `#241c16` (`--stage`, which the guide doesn't list).
- The style guide says "Do not introduce a second accent". The UI currently uses **three** accents: Burn (CUT, film, ready), Brass (links, call sheet, tutorial, sound/daily borders) and Carmine (danger, NEW TAKE, missed).
- **Fix:** Burn = the player's action (CUT, film, score highlights). Carmine = danger and the Talent only (lost, hostile, REC). Brass = interactive/secondary text only (links, focus rings), never borders. Replace `#ff4b3e`/`#ff3b2e` with Carmine, and `#8a7562`/`#b9a990` with Paper at 40%/60% alpha. Document `--stage` and `--muted` in the guide.

### 3.7 Icon styles
- Glyphs come from four sources: Unicode arrows (↗ ↓ ⟲ ⟳ ↸-style "⇱" in hints), "II" made from text for pause, a "▶" triangle on disclosures, "□" in the minimap, "✂" in the share line, and the lost arrow as a big text "➤" glyph with a text-shadow glow.
- **Fix:** one small inline-SVG icon set (pause, play, rotate L/R, zoom ±, share, settings, sound, chevron) with a 2 px stroke, square caps, and Paper/Burn fill. No external-link arrows on in-game actions.

### 3.8 Stamp and toast styles
- CUT stamp: Fraunces 20–24 px, Paper with an ink drop shadow, floats at the cut edge. Good.
- MISSED −2s: a Fraunces carmine *banner* (`.cut-banner`) in the upper frame.
- LOST HER: a Fraunces carmine box with a paper border, a sub-label and a bar, centred.
- Results "TAKE ENDED": Burn small caps over a Fraunces headline.
- The same carmine-box treatment is used for a minor penalty (missed) and the take-ending danger (lost), so severity doesn't read.
- **Fix:** three tiers. (1) **Cut stamp** (Fraunces, at the edge, about 0.9 s): success. (2) **Penalty tick** (Source Sans 600, Burn, beside the film counter, e.g. "−2s", about 0.8 s): minor. (3) **Danger frame state** (carmine inner glow on the frame edge nearest her + the edge arrow + the message line "Find her — 1.8 s"): critical. No centred boxes.

### 3.9 Animation easing
- There is a mix of `linear` (shake, not-ready, lock overlay), `ease-out` (stamp, banner, pop, kick, edge flash), `ease-in-out` alternates (lost pulse, threat pulse) and one `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.7,0.4,1)` (scrap). Pulses at 0.4–0.5 s alternate infinitely, two at once during lost-her (banner + film track + arrow).
- **Fix:** two tokens. `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.4, 1)` for anything entering or reacting, and `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.6, 0, 0.4, 1)` for state changes. Durations: 120 ms (UI state), 240 ms (panel), 900 ms (stamp). At most **one** infinite pulse on screen. Reduced motion keeps opacity changes and drops transforms (already partly done in `style.css:350`).

## 4. Redesign principles

**One language: the camera department.** During a take you are looking *through the camera*; everything else is paperwork you handle between takes.

1. **The viewfinder is the hero.** The gate fills the viewport, minus safe margins: about 92% of the short side on desktop and full width on phones. Nothing is drawn *over* the centre third of the frame.
2. **In-take HUD = four things, on the frame, like camera overlays.**
   - **Film / time**: a footage counter top-right of the frame ("104s"), plus the perforated film strip along the bottom edge as the remaining-film meter, advancing while rolling. One clock only.
   - **CUT charge**: a ring around the CUT button (touch) / a short meter by the bottom-left corner (desktop), with the READY state shown by fill, not a word.
   - **Take score**: top-left of the frame ("×1 100"), with the REC dot beside it.
   - **One message line**: bottom-centre inside the frame's lower safe band, max one line (two on phones), ≥14 px.
   - Plus contextual world markers: threat edge arrows, the lost-her edge state, cut stamps.
3. **Everything else leaves the take.** Stock, lens, daily, seed, best, take number go to the **slate** (pre-take). Sound, left hand, speed, reduced motion and the controls legend go to **pause › Settings**. Filmography, grade method, contact sheet, director's reel and replay go to **results › More**.
4. **One message channel.** `#lod`, `#message`, `#call-sheet`, the touch hint and tutorial steps all feed one prioritised queue: danger > tutorial step > incoming threat > shot-size advice > idle tip. It shows one line at a time, with a minimum display of 1.2 s and no duplicate within 4 s.
5. **Key legend only when needed:** during the first take (or until each verb has been used once), and permanently inside pause. On touch, the controls *are* the legend.
6. **Type.** Two fonts (Fraunces for display, Source Sans 3 for UI) and three sizes: 14 px (labels/body), 20 px (HUD numerals, message on desktop), and 40–64 px clamp (display: wordmark, stamp, results headline). Weights 400/600 only. Sentence case everywhere; caps only for ≤3-character tags (REC), with letter-spacing 0.08em.
7. **8 px grid.** All spacing is 8/16/24/32. Frame inset 16 px; touch targets ≥ 48 px with 8 px gaps.
8. **No borders.** Use tonal fills (Ink at 60–80% alpha) and space. The only lines are frame corners, meters and the focus ring (Brass, 2 px).
9. **Restrained palette:** Ink, Paper, Paper alpha tints, Burn (action), Carmine (danger/Talent/REC), Brass (focus/links). Nothing else in the UI.
10. **WCAG AA:** ≥4.5:1 for text under 18.66 px bold / 24 px regular; HUD text over the image gets an Ink 60% scrim chip. Verify Paper-on-Burn (the CUT label is Ink on Burn: OK) and Brass-on-Stage.
11. **Screens.**
    - **Title / slate:** CULL wordmark; primary **Play**, secondary **Daily**, tertiary **Settings**. Pressing Play shows the slate card (scene name, take number, best, stock + lens choice, seed small) for one tap or 1.5 s, then the clapper → rolling.
    - **Pause:** dim the frame to 40%, with Resume (primary), Restart take, Settings (sound, left hand, speed, reduced motion), Controls, Quit to title. Show the seed small at the bottom.
    - **Results ("Dailies"):** a paper card with the headline outcome ("She made it — 8 m" / "Lost her at 8 m"), one big score with grade, three stats (cuts, close calls, reels), primary **Another take** (Burn), secondary **Share**, and **More** (contact sheet, director's reel, replay, filmography, grade method). The HUD is hidden behind it.
12. **Mobile thumb zone.** CUT bottom-right (64 px, ring meter), HOLD bottom-left; mirrored by left hand. Zoom uses pinch plus an optional thin vertical rail on the CUT side. Rotate uses two-finger twist, with optional buttons in settings. Pause is top-right, outside the frame safe area. Controls sit **below** the frame, never over it; respect `env(safe-area-inset-*)`.
